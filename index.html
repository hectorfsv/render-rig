Made with this

Every one of these started as a sentence. Tap any picture to read the sentence that made it, then make your own from the same starting point.

Start here

You describe something, and a few minutes later it exists. A photo of a place that was never photographed. A short film of your dog on the moon. An old picture from a shoebox, sharp again.
There are four things this can do, and you pick one before you start:
VideoA short clip, with sound, made from a sentence.
ImageA single picture, made from a sentence.
ComposeHand it several photos, get one picture with all of them in it.
DesignArtwork with your words set into it — a flyer, a poster, an invitation, a meme.
UpscaleTake a picture you already have and make it bigger and clearer.
Each one uses a different piece of software behind the scenes, and they are good at different things — but you never have to choose between them. Pick what you want to make, and the right one is already loaded.
Just write like you are talking to a person. Your words get expanded into a longer, more detailed brief before anything is made, so you do not need special phrasing or a list of keywords. Say what you want to see. Words you want on something — a sign, a shirt, a screen — go in quotes, and they are rendered exactly as typed, typos included.
Every run costs real money — a few cents for a picture, more for video. The exact price is on the button before you press it, and it is never a surprise. If nothing comes back, you are not charged. Refusals, crashes and runs you give up on are all free.

Your first four runs

In this order. It takes about ten minutes and costs well under a dollar, and by the end you will know how the whole thing behaves.
  1. Pick Upscale and give it any photo from your phone. $0.08, nothing to write, back in under a minute. Start here because there is nothing to get wrong — it just proves everything works.
  2. Now Image. One honest sentence about something you would like to see. $0.06. When it arrives, look at the bottom of the picture: there is a number labelled seed. Tap it.
  3. Now write only what should change and run it again. Because you tapped the seed, the rig hands Grok the prompt it wrote for that picture and Grok changes just that — so whatever moves in the picture is what your change did. This is the trick that teaches you the most, and it is the reason the seed exists.
  4. Finally Video, set to 3 seconds with sound off — $0.336, the cheapest real clip. Video is the expensive one: fifteen seconds with sound is $2.52, so learn on short ones first.

Seed

You will never write a sentence detailed enough to describe a whole picture. You might ask for a man in a red coat on a beach — but which way is he facing? Morning or evening? Is the beach empty? Something has to decide all of that, and that something is a random number called the seed.
Think of it as which shuffle of the deck you were dealt. The same words with the same seed always give the same picture. The same words with a different seed give a completely different take on the same idea — still a man in a red coat on a beach, but a new one.
Here is why you should care. Say a picture comes back that you nearly love, and you want the coat to be blue. Change the word and you also get a new random seed — so you get a different beach, a different man, a different everything. The thing you liked is gone.
So keep the seed and change the word. Now the only thing that can move is the coat. That is how you steer a picture instead of gambling on it, and it is the most useful habit here.
In practice: when a picture arrives, its seed is shown just underneath it. Tap that number and it drops into the Seed box, locked. From then on the prompt box is for the change alone — make the coat blue — and Grok edits the prompt it already wrote instead of writing a new one. Run the same words again and the same picture comes back. The padlock holds it there, the dice rolls a fresh one, and an empty box means a new random seed every time — which is what you want while you are still exploring.
Only Image and Compose have a seed. Video and Upscale do not, so the box is not shown for those two.

Directing a clip

Direct it shot by shot. One paragraph asks the model to guess where a move happens; a shot list tells it. The durations have to add up to the clip length and the rig will not let you submit if they do not. This is the real answer to a camera move Kling keeps ignoring.
Name a character from the tray. A front-on picture plus up to three other angles becomes @Element1, and you call it that in the prompt. Use it to hold the same face, or the same jacket, across a whole clip. Giving it a voice makes it speak — and moves the clip to $0.196 a second.
End on picks which source is the last frame. Ending on the opening frame gives a clip that loops with no cut, and it is free.
Prompt adherence, under Advanced, decides how literally your words are followed. Turn it up and the clip sticks closer to what you asked for and drifts further from the starting photo. Leave it alone unless a camera move keeps getting ignored — then it is worth a try.
All of this is free. Shot lists, characters, end frames, adherence and keep-out words change the clip, never the bill.

The price is set by what comes out, so the meter works out the exact figure from your source instead of guessing. Enlarge by is not limited to whole numbers — a 12 MP photo at 1.4× is $0.08, at 2× it is $0.16. Dial it down until the meter drops a tier.
Match the model to the source. Standard for photographs, High Fidelity when there is fine detail worth protecting, Low Resolution for something small or soft, CGI for renders and illustration, Text Refine for screenshots and type, Recovery for something damaged or heavily compressed.
The generative models — Redefine, Wonder, Wonder 3 and Standard MAX — invent new detail instead of rebuilding what is there, and fal publishes no price for them. The meter says "from" and means it. Redefine takes a prompt of its own, which is how you tell it what to bring back.
Faces are enhanced by default, at Topaz's own strength. Turn it off for a subject with no face or it will find one anyway.

Working in the console

The stage keeps your last result while you set up the next run, so you can look at what you got while you change what you asked for.
Everything you make drops into the session strip. Tap one to bring it back to the stage; the small arrow sends it into the source tray, so you can build on it, upscale it, or make it a clip's opening frame.
The strip survives a refresh, a crash and a browser restart, because it is stored on this device. It does not follow you to another device, and Clear empties it.
You can close the tab mid-render. Come back within an hour and the rig picks the job back up where it left off, so a paid render is no longer lost by closing a tab.
The passphrase is remembered for two hours, then the rig asks again. It spends real money, so it should not stay unlocked forever.
