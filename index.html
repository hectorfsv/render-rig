What this is

Four jobs, one console. Video is Kling 3.0 Pro, Image is Krea, Compose is Nano Banana Pro, Upscale is Topaz. Picking the job picks the engine, so every control you can see belongs to the model you are actually using.
You only pay for output that arrives. A refusal, a crash, or a run you walk away from costs nothing. fal bills on delivery.
Whatever you type is rewritten by Grok into a fuller brief before the model sees it, so plain English beats keyword soup. Say what you want, not what you think a model wants to hear.

Your first four runs

  1. Start on Upscale with a photo off your phone. $0.08, no prompt, back in under a minute — the cheapest way to prove the whole chain works.
  2. Then Image, one honest sentence, $0.06. Write down the seed it comes back with.
  3. Run it again with that seed locked and one word changed. Whatever moves is what that word did. This is the single most useful habit in the rig.
  4. Then Video at 3 seconds, audio off — $0.336, the cheapest real clip. Fifteen seconds with audio is $2.52, so learn on the short ones.

The four jobs

Video$0.336+Kling 3.0 Pro. 3 to 15 seconds, billed per second. Sound written from your words.
Image$0.06Krea 2 Large. Words to picture. Draws real people and copyrighted characters without complaint.
Compose$0.15Nano Banana Pro. Merges the photos you hand it into one picture. Refuses likenesses.
Upscale$0.08Topaz. Enlarges something you already have. No prompt.
Image and Compose are not a cheap and an expensive version of the same thing. Krea is the one that will draw anyone; Nano Banana Pro is the one that can combine your photos. Pick on what you need, not on price.
If Nano Banana Pro refuses your subject, Krea is the fallback and it costs less. Nothing unfiltered beats Krea at this price — that was measured, so there is no better model waiting to be found.

Seed

Every render starts from a random number. That number is the seed, and it settles everything you did not specify: which way a face turns, where the light falls, how a crowd is arranged.
Same seed and same prompt gives the same picture, every time. Change the seed and you get a completely different take on the same description.
That is what the lock is for. Run something you like, lock the seed, then change one word. Whatever moves is what that word actually did. Without the lock you change a word, get a different picture, and never learn which of the two caused it.
The dice rolls a new number by hand; leave the field empty and every run rolls its own. Image and Compose both take a seed. Video and Upscale have none.

Image — Krea

Creativity is how far Krea may drift from your words. Raw takes them literally, High reinterprets freely. Medium is the model's own default and the place to start.
Eight aspect ratios, including 2.35:1 — anamorphic cinema, the widest shape in the rig. Only Krea offers it.
Krea uses the seed, and it uses your sources. An earlier version of this guide said it ignored both. That was wrong about the model, and the rig was not sending either one. Both are wired now.
It treats sources as style references, not as content — it borrows their look, it does not put them in the picture. Tick Use sources as style references and set the strength. That is the only thing on Krea that costs extra, and it is half a cent. To actually combine photos, use Compose.

Compose — Nano Banana Pro

Every source in the tray is merged into one picture. Up to 14 of them, and the number you put in is free — you are billed per picture that comes out. Give it a face, a jacket and a room, and ask for all three together.
Order matters to the model and the tray is numbered, so describe them the same way: the man in image 1 wearing the coat from image 2.
2K is the floor worth using. fal bills 1K and 2K the same, so there is no 1K button — it could only ever have cost you detail for free. 4K is double, and earns it only for print or a big screen.
Under Advanced: Content filter runs 1 to 6, and every refusal we have ever had ran at the default 4. It has never been tried higher. Reckon on about 44 seconds of rendering per second of video. A 10 second clip is roughly 7 minutes.
The sound is written from your words. A prompt with no sound cues comes back nearly silent. Name the song, the line of dialogue, the room tone — whatever you want to hear, say it.
Start from a photo and the clip copies that photo's shape. The aspect ratio buttons disappear, because Kling has no such setting when it starts from an image. If you want a 9:16 clip, start from a 9:16 picture.

Directing a clip

Direct it shot by shot. One paragraph asks the model to guess where a move happens; a shot list tells it. The durations have to add up to the clip length and the rig will not let you submit if they do not. This is the real answer to a camera move Kling keeps ignoring.
Name a character from the tray. A front-on picture plus up to three other angles becomes @Element1, and you call it that in the prompt. Use it to hold the same face, or the same jacket, across a whole clip. Giving it a voice makes it speak — and moves the clip to $0.196 a second.
End on picks which source is the last frame. Ending on the opening frame gives a clip that loops with no cut, and it is free.
Prompt adherence, under Advanced, is the lever never yet tried on the orbit problem. Higher sticks closer to your words and further from the source picture. Leave it alone and Kling uses its own default.
All of this is free. Shot lists, characters, end frames, adherence and keep-out words change the clip, never the bill.

Upscale — Topaz

Up to 24 MP$0.08A 2K picture at 2× lands here.
Up to 48 MP$0.162K at 3×.
Up to 96 MP$0.322K at 4×.
The price is set by what comes out, so the meter works out the exact figure from your source instead of guessing. Enlarge by is not limited to whole numbers — a 12 MP photo at 1.4× is $0.08, at 2× it is $0.16. Dial it down until the meter drops a tier.
Match the model to the source. Standard for photographs, High Fidelity when there is fine detail worth protecting, Low Resolution for something small or soft, CGI for renders and illustration, Text Refine for screenshots and type, Recovery for something damaged or heavily compressed.
The generative models — Redefine, Wonder, Wonder 3 and Standard MAX — invent new detail instead of rebuilding what is there, and fal publishes no price for them. The meter says "from" and means it. Redefine takes a prompt of its own, which is how you tell it what to bring back.
Faces are enhanced by default, at Topaz's own strength. Turn it off for a subject with no face or it will find one anyway.

Working in the console

The stage keeps your last result while you set up the next run, so you can look at what you got while you change what you asked for.
Everything you make drops into the session strip. Tap one to bring it back to the stage; the small arrow sends it into the source tray, so you can build on it, upscale it, or make it a clip's opening frame.
The strip survives a refresh, a crash and a browser restart, because it is stored on this device. It does not follow you to another device, and Clear empties it.
You can close the tab mid-render. Come back within an hour and the rig picks the job back up where it left off, so a paid render is no longer lost by closing a tab.
The passphrase is remembered for two hours, then the rig asks again. It spends real money, so it should not stay unlocked forever.
